##### WORKSHOP HIGHLIGHTS & KEY TAKEAWAYS 📋 
---
##### 1. AI Literacies for Educators
Understanding what Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLMs) can and cannot do:
- **Demystifying AI:** Generative vs. Traditional AI and how LLMs work.
- **Myths vs. Reality:** AI is a force multiplier for teachers, not a replacement for human empathy, context, and mentorship.
- **Handling Hallucinations & Bias:** Validating AI outputs and teaching students critical evaluation.

##### 2. The 6-D Framework for Classroom Use
A practical model for integrating AI across daily educational workflows:
- **Discover:** Researching topics and gathering diverse viewpoints.
- **Design:** Crafting lesson plans, curriculum outlines, and rubrics.
- **Differentiate:** Adapting reading levels and personalizing learning materials for students with different learning styles.
- **Draft:** Generating starter prompts, sample essays, and discussion questions.
- **Deliver:** Interactive quizzes, flashcards, and multimodal aids.
- **Debrief:** Analyzing assessment patterns and giving targeted feedback.

##### 3. Prompt Engineering for Teachers: The R-C-T-F Framework
Get accurate and relevant responses from AI by structuring your prompts:
* **R — Role:** Who should the AI act as? (e.g., *"Act as an experienced middle-school science educator..."*)
* **C — Context:** What background information does it need? (e.g., *"Grade 7, unit on photosynthesis, mixed-ability classroom..."*)
* **T — Task:** What specifically should it produce? (e.g., *"Create a 5-question multiple choice quiz with answer explanations..."*)
* **F — Format:** How should the output look? (e.g., *"Format as a table with columns for Question, Options, Answer, and Hint."*)

##### 4. Ethics, Academic Integrity & Policy
- Addressing AI-assisted student work constructively.
- Focusing on process-driven assessment rather than solely final output.
- Drafting school-wide AI usage guidelines and classroom contracts.
